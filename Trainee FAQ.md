# Trainee FAQ

This page will help you find some answers to your questions about CodeYourFuture (CYF) and our courses. 


## I want to know more about the CodeYourFuture courses

- **Am I eligible for the CodeYourFuture (CYF) courses?**
    
    CodeYourFuture exists to support disadvantaged groups into thriving tech careers. Disadvantage can take many forms; if your specific disadvantage is not mentioned below, please apply for the Intro to Digital course.
    
    Suitability identifies if you are a match for the CodeYourFuture cultural and population criteria.
    
    Eligibility identifies if you have the minimum qualifications ot be allowed to join Intro to Programming onwards.
    
    **Suitability**
    
    Have you experienced one or more of the following:
    
    - Poverty
    - Long-term unemployment
    - Disability
    - Homelessness
    - Refuge
    - Asylum
    - Forced migration
    - Prison
    - Single parenthood
    - Social care
    
    **Eligibility**
    
    - Are you over 18 years old?
    - Are you on a low income?
    - Are you looking to start a career in tech?
    - Do you live within 90 minutes by public transport of:
        - Sheffield, UK
        - Glasgow, UK
        - Manchester, UK
        - Birmingham, UK
        - London, UK
        - Cape Town, ZA
- **Do applicants need to have the right to work in the UK?**
    
    No. Having the right to work in the UK is not a requirement to sign up for our vocational courses.
    
- **I do not have a work visa or ID. Can I still take this course?**
    
    Yes, you can take our course independent of your legal status in the country.
    
- **Why should I take a course at CodeYourFuture?**
    
    **Free, flexible and no experience needed.**
    
    - 96% of students have little to no coding experience before starting
    
    **Learn skills you need to get a good job.**
    
    - Learn the technical and employability skills that are in demand from the local job market.
    
    **1:1 personal support and guidance.**
    
    - A community of supportive professionals will help you from your first day until you get a job in tech.

On the 1st May, you submit all coursework and PRs, you go to the demos, book a pair-programming session, and you tell everyone on Slack you’d like to join Checkpoint in June. From the beginning of the course, trainees are expected to prepare and take ownership of their learning before they attend in-person sessions.

    https://youtu.be/r2b7GeuqkPc

    Time is very important at CYF. We try our best to use everyone’s time as well as we can. We want to use our time together to help learners with the things they can’t do at home: debugging, pair programming, code review, and building projects.

- **I want to know more about the Intro to Programming (ITP) course.**
    
    CodeYourFuture's Intro to Programming course is a comprehensive, self-paced program designed to equip you with essential programming skills.
    
    This course will give you a solid foundation in programming, preparing you for further learning and real-world projects.
    
    The course is 13 weeks long, with the potential for extra tracks.
    
    The course is divided into 4 modules, each containing 3 sprints.
    
    By the end of the course, you'll be able to:
    
    - Predict and explain program execution
    - Refactor code based on new requirements
    - Write composable functions
    - Turn business requirements into test cases
    - Design applications based on user stories
    - Work with datasets
    - Verify web application accessibility
    - Understand processes and file descriptors
    - Use GitHub effectively, including pull requests
- **Tell me more about the Checkpoint**
    
    CodeYourFuture's Checkpoint is an intensive 22-day assessment course designed for trainees who have completed the Intro to Programming (ITP) and wish to join the Software Development Course (SDC).
    
    By completing the Checkpoint, you'll gain hands-on experience in project development, teamwork, and independent coding. You'll also improve your ability to articulate technical concepts, a crucial skill in the tech industry.
    
    During the Checkpoint, you'll work on three projects: two group projects and one solo project.
    
    For each project, your tasks will be to:
    
    1. Build a complete project that meets all requirements, including tests
    2. Develop a thorough understanding of all the code in your project, as you'll need to explain it
    
    The Checkpoint aims to identify candidates ready to train as professional software engineers. The program assesses your ability to:
    
    1. Collaborate in a team to build working software with tests
    2. Write simple code independently
    3. Clearly explain your work in English
    
    These core goals reflect the skills needed for success in the CYF Software Development Course and as a future software engineer.
    
- **Can you tell me more about your Software Development Course (SDC)?**
    
    **The Software Development Programme is 12 weeks, part-time.**
    
    The Software Development Programme (SDC) has shifted focus from traditional web development to prioritising systems engineering, infrastructure, and operations.
    
    This involves challenging participants with more complex tasks in areas such as systems work, Linux, troubleshooting, data structures, and algorithms. Regular demonstrations allow participants to showcase their progress to the community, while the integration of existing systems courses provides additional learning opportunities.
    
    The ultimate goal is to cultivate critical thinking and problem-solving skills that will help you stand out as a developer.

## I have questions about coming to the classes

- **Are the courses online or in-person?**
    
    It’s a mix:
    
    - **The first course, Intro to Digital is online.** We also host in-person and online workshops for this course and recommend people attend at least one in-person workshop if possible, to connect with our community and assess the CodeYourFuture organisation first hand.
    - **From the second course onwards,** participants are generally expected to come to classes in person on Saturdays at one of our centres. Coursework is also completed during the week.
    - **We can fund transport** for those who progress to the in-person courses on Saturdays (Introduction to Programming, Checkpoint, Software Engineering).
- **Which day and time are the classes?**
    
    Attendance at classes varies according to the course.
    
    - **Intro to Digital:** *optional* classes in-person on Saturdays (from 10 am) and online on Wednesdays evening ****(7 pm- 9 pm)
    - **Intro to Programming:** *mandatory* in-person classes on Saturdays (10 am - 5 pm)
    - Checkpoint: *mandatory* classes on Saturdays (10 am - 5 pm) - aim to be in-person, *might be online if we do not have enough people in your region*
    - **Software Development Course**: *mandatory* classes on Saturdays (10 am - 5 pm) - aim to be in-person, *might be online if we do not have enough people in your region*
    - **Launch**: *mandatory* online classes on Saturdays (10 am - 5 pm)

## Eligibility questions

- **What is the eligibility check and process?**
    
    You can read about what we consider for eligibility on this question.
    
    The process is:
    
    1. **Pass Eligibility survey:** in which we calculate if you are from a low-income background - read more on this doc - and if you are too far to attend our in-person classes
    2. **Pass Duolingo English Test**: for now, we ask everyone to take the test - no matter where you are born, what you studied, etc. You will NOT pay for this test; Duolingo is our partner and gives it to us for free.
    3. **Sign the trainee agreement**
    
    This process will always have a deadline to be done, which is communicated via email and Slack - so keep an eye on both, check your junk folder regularly, and mark our emails as trusted so you don’t miss out on it.
    
- **If I have questions about next steps or the process, what’s the first place I should check?**
    
    The CYF Portal is your first call for information. It shows all steps you have to take, clarifies the next steps, and has any other information. 
    
- **What’s the difference between the Duolingo English Test I took during Intro to Digital and the certified test you ask me to take for eligibility?**
    
    During Intro to Digital, you use the ***practice*** test.
    
    For eligibility, you will do the ***Duolingo English Test (DET)***, which is a formal test, with many checks (ID, rules for laptop/cameras), and you get a certificate too!
    
- **I do not have a quiet space to take the Duolingo English Test. Can you help?**
    
    Post on your community Slack channel (i.e. #london-community) so you can go to our offices on Saturday and take the test from there. Many of our regions also have extra laptops you can use for that in the location.
    
- **I am a native speaker and/or have an English certificate. Can I waive the Duolingo test?**
    
    No, right now we only accept the Duolingo English test to ensure we have a standard way of evaluating all trainees equally. 
    
    The test is free, and if you have good English, it will be easy for you to pass it. 
    
- **I took the Duolingo test, but it was not complete or uncertified.**
    
    Your first point of contact is the Duolingo support team. They can look into the reasons this happened and hopefully give you a second chance to take the test.

    ## Improve your English
    
    **Speaking and Listening**
    
    1. Speak English every day with one native speaker.
    2. Speak for at least 15 minutes each day.
    3. Download the Duolingo app on your phone and practice your English.
    4. Come to CYF socials and drop-in workshops and talk to us!
    
    **Writing and Reading**
    
    1. Take the PD Coursework seriously and write without using AI.
    2. Download the Duolingo app on your phone and practice your English.
    3. Read a page of a book, online article or anything of your interest and choice in English.
    4. Take 15 min a day and write a short text. Check it with a Grammar AI. Understand what your mistakes were. Try re-writing it until it is correct.

    - When watching TV or movies, put the English captions on so you can follow along.
    - Join an English language exchange and share your own language with a native speaker.
    - Improve your Technical English by attending tech meetups

- **What is Career Mentorship? Why do I need a career mentor?**
    
    Once you are in Intro to Programming, you should have a career mentor.
    
    Career mentorship is a collaborative and trust-based partnership to support you in your career development. A volunteer with professional experience will work with you, a CodeYourFuture trainee, in a 1-2-1 relationship. 
    
    The goal is for the volunteer to guide your professional journey. They can help you with strategic ideas, industry perspective and share their experience so you can achieve your career goals.
    
    *This is **NOT** a session to discuss technical questions or review a pull request. That’s what pair programming is there for.*
    
    > **Format**: a meeting that can be weekly, bi-weekly or monthly, depending on both availability and goals
    > 
    
    > **Type of meeting**: online or in-person
    > 
    
    > **Frequency**: at least 6 sessions, but you can agree to more
    > 
    
    As a mentee, you must:
    
    - **Define what you want to achieve:** before requesting a mentor, be clear on what you want to achieve. A vague request gets a vague result. A specific goal (e.g., "I want to refine my portfolio for junior dev roles") leads to a much more successful match.
    - **Own the Logistics:** You are the driver of this relationship. It’s your responsibility to book the sessions with your mentor. Don't wait for them to chase you - if you don't book it, it won't happen.
    - **Respect the volunteer’s time:** Your mentor is donating their time. If you absolutely must cancel, do it, whenever possible, days in advance. Not showing up is very disrespectful to the volunteer and to CodeYourFuture.
    - **Keep it Professional, Not Personal:** Use each meeting to focus on career direction, skill gaps, and industry insights. Don’t expect the mentor to fix things for you. You own your journey and should be prepared to lead on it
    - **Come Prepared to Work:** Never show up to a session empty-handed. Bring specific questions, be ready to be challenged, and commit to following up on any actions in the previous meeting.
    
    ---
    
    > **Pro-Tip:** If you're feeling overwhelmed or too busy to commit to all 6 sessions right now, it’s better to wait. Mentorship is a privilege - commit to it when you can give it your best.
    > 
    
    <aside>
    
    **Ending a mentorship**
    
    This can happen once you fulfil your work, but also when you just don’t click with the mentor you are assigned to.
    
    Either way, thank them for their time and make sure to let them know you won’t be booking any time with them anymore.
